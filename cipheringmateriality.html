Inspired by Brickmakers in Nashik, India

Project Brief
Speculative Study: Brick & Mesh explores the relationship between traditional craft and modern technology through the study of earth as a building material. Inspired by brick makers in Nashik, India, the project examines how rammed earth can interact with contemporary materials like wire mesh. It focuses on three key areas: Material composition: experimenting with brick ingredients, ratios, and drying processes, including the use of waste materials like ash and plant fibres. Brick design and prototyping: developing interlocking bricks without mortar through 3D printing, plaster casting, and rammed earth modelling. Speculative construction: reimagining how new structures can build upon existing ones, merging rammed earth and wire mesh through computational design and fibrous weaving techniques. Overall, the project blends traditional material knowledge with digital fabrication to explore new possibilities in sustainable construction and material transitions.
Phase I: Material Composition

Phase I involved experimenting with rammed earth brick compositions, the emulsions included different amounts of earth, water, ash and plant fibres. Ash (sourced from the remnants of Mercurio's pizza oven) proved to be the most effective ingredient for binding the rammed earth brick.
Phase II: Tesselating Brick Design
Tap image to zoom ++
The main goal was to achieve a brick design that would not require a mechanical or chemical fastener. To achieve this, several prototype iterations were made using different fabrication techniques. Starting from an initial plaster prototype moulded in a silicone mould. The angular edges and hook design created issues with the casting process, this implied the need for a mould that can be easily separated.
Phase III: RAMMED EARTH Brick Design Prototype
Tap image to zoom ++
Moving into the rammed earth material and a revised CNC milled wood mould (separating into two), the angular geometry design of the brick clashed with the material properties of the rammed earth. This implied the need for a more rounded, organic design.
Phase IV: RAMMED EARTH Finalised Brick Design
Tap image to zoom ++
Learning from the previous iterations, a finalised rounded brick design with a CNC milled (now easily separated into four) would mould. This phase created a succesfully designed brick module using sustainable alternative materials.
